Battleships

Battleships for several players over a network, played in the terminal.

A pair project for Programming Distributed Systems. We wrote Battleships for three players in Python: a server holds the boards and runs the game, and each player connects to it over TCP from a terminal. Everyone guesses a number at the start, and the closest guess goes first.

Improvements since the course

The course version worked as a sketch, but a typo in a coordinate crashed the server, two messages could arrive stuck together because TCP has no message boundaries, and the target of each shot was picked at random. I rebuilt it. Every message is now one line, and every answer is checked, with a wrong one explained and asked again. Everyone places ships at the same time, you choose which opponent to fire at, and each turn shows your fleet and every opponent's known board. Every hit, sunk ship and knocked-out player is announced to everyone.

The server now uses asyncio and copes with players leaving: whoever disconnects is out, and the game carries on without them. Games take 2 to 6 players, and computer players can fill empty seats. They only use what every player can see, hunting around a hit and otherwise firing in a checkerboard pattern. That sinks a fleet in about 52 shots on average, against about 95 for random firing.

The game rules are now separate from the networking and covered by 37 pytest tests. They include full games against a real server over TCP, with scripted players who make typing mistakes and drop out mid-game.
