Forest fire spread simulation

A Python model of how a wildfire spreads, with wind and firebreaks.

A project for a modelling and simulation course. The forest is a 50 by 50 grid of trees and empty ground. Each step, a tree next to a fire has a chance of catching, burning trees burn out, and wind makes the fire more likely to spread in one direction.

You can start a fire by clicking a tree, switch wind and a firebreak on and off, and run a forecast that simulates 50 possible futures to show where the fire is most likely to reach.

Improvements since the course

The forecast now shows the chance of each cell burning across all runs, an optional random seed makes runs repeatable, and the default wind direction matches the controls.

The step function now updates the whole grid at once with NumPy array operations instead of looping over every cell in Python. That made it about 130 times faster on the 50 by 50 grid and about 360 times faster on a 200 by 200 grid, so larger forests stay interactive. The model is now separate from the Matplotlib interface and covered by seeded pytest tests, for example that a fire cannot cross a full-width firebreak without wind.
